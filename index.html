How to read this view

Card guide

What each card measures, how it is calculated, and what to check before quoting it. Every value responds to the filters at the top of the page.

Reviews Snapshot

Counts project-lead submissions, not people. One submission covers one resource for one engagement window, so a resource reviewed twice adds two reviews.

Now showing: 2 reviews from 2 resources

Competency score Snapshot

Each competency answer becomes a number (Poor 1, Fair 2, Good 3, Very good 4, Excellent 5), then all six competencies across all filtered reviews are averaged into one figure out of 5.0.

Now showing: 4.0 of 5.0

Team recommendation Snapshot

Averages the 0–10 answer to how likely the lead is to recommend working with this engagement team. Read it as a likelihood rating on its own scale, not as a net promoter score.

Now showing: 8.0 of 10

Future project fit KPI card

The share of filtered reviews where the lead would take the resource onto another project. Anything below 100% should be traced to a specific row in the table before it is acted on.

Now showing: 2 of 2 recommended

Represented counts KPI cards

Distinct resources and distinct projects behind the reviews. Comparing these against the review count separates feedback volume from how much of the roster is actually covered.

Now showing: 2 resources across 1 project

Top rating KPI card

The rating wording chosen most often across every competency answer. It shows where the ratings cluster, which the averaged score alone can hide.

Now showing: Very good

Competency chart Chart

One bar per competency, averaged across filtered reviews on the same 1–5 scale. Bars are green from 4.5, orange from 4.0, and gold below 4.0, so softer competencies stand out without reading the axis.

Recommendation donut Chart

Splits filtered reviews into recommended and not recommended for a future project. The centre figure repeats the future-fit percentage so the chart can be read on its own.

Reviewed resources Table

One row per submission with the engagement window, that review's own competency average, and its team score. Ratings are lead-reported and free-text comments are deliberately excluded, so treat the numbers as directional.

How to read this view

Card guide

What each card measures, how it is calculated, and what to check before quoting it. Every value responds to the filters at the top of the page.

Responses Snapshot

Counts internal-resource submissions. A resource who submitted more than once is kept as separate responses rather than merged, so this can exceed the number of people.

Now showing: 2 responses from 2 resources

Experience score Snapshot

Each answer becomes a number (Poor 1, Fair 2, Good 3, Very good 4, Excellent 5), then all seven engagement dimensions across all filtered responses are averaged into one figure out of 5.0.

Now showing: 4.4 of 5.0

Project recommendation Snapshot

Averages the 0–10 answer to how likely the resource is to recommend working on this project. It is a separate question from the ratings, so it can move independently of the experience score.

Now showing: 9.0 of 10

Work with client again KPI card

The share of filtered responses willing to return to this client. Read it next to the challenge chart, since a resource can report friction and still say yes.

Now showing: 2 of 2 would return

Strongest dimension KPI card

The highest-scoring of the seven dimensions under the current filters, with its score. Use it to name what the next roster should preserve, not as an overall verdict.

Now showing: Client professionalism at 5.0

Challenges reported KPI card

Counts selections, not people. The question allows several answers per response, so this total can be higher than the response count and is not a percentage.

Now showing: 3 selections across 2 responses

Competency chart Chart

One bar per engagement dimension, averaged across filtered responses on the same 1–5 scale. Bars are green from 4.5, orange from 4.0, and gold below 4.0, so weaker conditions stand out immediately.

Challenge chart Chart

Bar length is the number of responses naming that challenge, sorted most to least frequent. A challenge named by most respondents is the practical watch item for the next engagement.

Now showing: Lack of clear communication in 2 of 2

Internal-resource responses Table

One row per submission with track, team lead, that response's own experience average, and its project score. Emails and free-text comments are excluded, so the table stays shareable.
